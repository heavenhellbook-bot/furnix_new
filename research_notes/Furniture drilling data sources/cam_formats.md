# Furniture drilling data exchange and production outputs

## Vendor data and obtaining drilling information

### Takeaway
Furniture designers can obtain real drilling locations and related manufacturing data from fitting manufacturers and their CAD/CAM partners, but access is product-, configuration-, and software-dependent. These sources provide authoritative inputs for a FURNIX rule model; they do not imply that a drawing or generic exchange file is ready to run on any CNC.

### Cited findings
- **Blum BXF** means Blum Exchange Format. Blum says a BXF contains fitting information and manufacturing information for wooden parts, including cutting dimensions and drilling positions. It is generated by Blum’s Product or Cabinet Configurator and can be imported into CAD software or used to produce furniture on MINIPRESS with EASYSTICK. Blum says implementation details for software partners are available through a Blum contact—not as a public interface specification. [Blum FAQ](https://www.blum.com/eu/en/services/faq/)
- Blum’s CAD/CAM service offers product and configured-application data, 2D production drawings for drilling positions, and CAM machining macros. It describes a “wide range of WOP formats” compatible with CNC machines from “most manufacturers”—not all machines. [Blum CAD/CAM Data Service](https://www.blum.com/eu/en/services/industrial-production/cad-cam-dataservice/)
- Blum identifies multiple distinct software integrations: an embedded fittings configurator, a BXF interface, and fitting libraries. Its partner page also describes collision-checked solutions and assembly, parts-list, fitting, and wooden-part data. Those are different integration paths, not one universal interface. [Blum CAD/CAM software interface](https://www.blum.com/eu/en/services/industrial-production/cad-cam-interface/)
- Hettich says its CAD partners can provide original drawings including drilling data and master-data logic for **many** fittings inside partner material libraries. Hettich’s partner list includes iFurn. Its CAD service also offers multiple direct-download formats, including 2D/3D DWG/DXF, plus PDFs; it describes these as CAD data and resources for planning, dimension comparison, collision checks, and determining drilling points. [Hettich CAD](https://www.hettich.com/en-us/services/hettich-cad)
- Hettich’s partner-availability page gives examples of richer integrations: for example, certain CAD libraries can insert fittings with processing, while Hettich directs users to iFurn for some connector data. Coverage varies by CAD partner and fitting family. [Hettich CAD partner packages](https://www.hettich.com/en-us/services/hettich-cad/cad-data-packages)
- iFurn describes digitizing original supplier product data and making it available through its cloud/service ecosystem. Its current iFurn Composer page says a selected fitting set can be downloaded with a coordinate cross, CAD model, and CAM data; it also advertises catalog data for individual fittings. The same page identifies machine use for HOMAG machines that are “tapio-ready” with powerControl V2—so a claim of CAD-system portability is not the same as universal controller compatibility. [iFurn](https://www.ifurn.net/); [iFurn Composer at tapio](https://www.tapio.one/ifurn-composer)

### Inferences
- Treat Blum BXF, Hettich partner libraries, and iFurn as **supplier-data channels**: potentially reliable sources of fitting-specific drilling rules, but subject to the supplied product variant, revision, configuration, and integration.
- WOP/CAM macros are production-oriented outputs or programming aids, not a machinery-neutral standard. Blum’s own wording—multiple WOP formats compatible with “most” machines—signals that format and capability depend on the target ecosystem.
- Capture the source rule and its scope when importing data. A manufacturer-verified rule for one SKU or configuration should not be silently generalized to another fitting or cabinet arrangement.

### Gaps
- Blum’s public FAQ says the detailed BXF interface specification is available from Blum, but does not publish the schema. Obtain the current licensed specification and sample files before implementing an importer.
- The public sources establish that Hettich and iFurn provide data and integrations, but do not expose a complete, open drilling-rule schema or prove every data package contains machining operations for every fitting.
- The iFurn Composer page is current vendor information, but its precise licensing, file payload, and machine eligibility should be checked for the intended market/account and target workflow.

## Exchange formats, CNC formats, and what standards cover

### Takeaway
A neutral project model should preserve both geometry and explicit machining semantics. STEP, DXF, and CSV can support exchange or documentation, but their use alone does not ensure another application understands the intended drilling operations. ISO standards reviewed here cover product-data exchange syntax and NC program format—not a universal furniture drilling-map schema.

### Cited findings
- **STEP / ISO 10303-21** specifies a clear-text exchange structure for transferring product data described in EXPRESS between computer systems. Its scope is an exchange format; it does not by itself establish that a recipient will interpret furniture-specific hole types, drilling faces, fitting rules, or machining intent. [ISO 10303-21:2016](https://www.iso.org/standard/63141.html)
- **ISO 6983-1:2009** covers program format and address words for positioning, line motion, and contouring NC systems. ISO describes interchangeability as applying between machines of the same classification by type, process, function, size, and accuracy. That is a narrower target than arbitrary cross-vendor CNC portability. [ISO 6983-1:2009](https://www.iso.org/standard/34608.html)
- Blum’s service describes CAM macros and a range of WOP formats; iFurn describes CAM data and separately specifies eligibility for certain HOMAG/tapio machines. These vendor descriptions are evidence that real production outputs and integrations are ecosystem-specific. [Blum CAD/CAM Data Service](https://www.blum.com/eu/en/services/industrial-production/cad-cam-dataservice/); [iFurn Composer](https://www.tapio.one/ifurn-composer)
- CSV is a text representation with no inherent furniture-drilling semantics. The commonly referenced CSV format document addresses record and field syntax, not a standard definition of hole diameter, depth, face, or coordinate frame. [RFC 4180](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4180)

### Inferences
- **DXF:** useful for 2D part outlines, dimensioned drawings, and visual hole maps. A circle or point in a drawing does not, by itself, unambiguously state whether a hole is blind or through, which face it is drilled from, its depth, tool, or machining sequence. DXF can carry conventions or attributes, but those conventions must be documented and understood by the receiving software.
- **CSV:** useful for explicit tables of operations if FURNIX defines and versions a schema. Include units, part identifiers, face/coordinate-frame identifiers, operation type, and field definitions; do not call an arbitrary table a standardized CNC file.
- **STEP:** appropriate for exchanging product geometry when the receiving application supports the relevant STEP profile. Do not assume a STEP solid model communicates a fitting’s machining rules or generates a toolpath.
- **CNC/WOP output:** should be a separate postprocessing stage for a selected controller, machine, and setup. Do not emit generic G-code or label a machine-neutral project as a production program.
- No universal furniture drilling-map or hole-operation exchange standard was identified in the official vendor and standards sources reviewed. This is a bounded finding, not proof that no industry, regional, or licensed schema exists.

### Gaps
- The public sources reviewed do not define all supported DXF entities, CAM macro semantics, or a full vendor-to-controller compatibility matrix. Confirm those details with each receiving CAD/CAM vendor and machine supplier.
- Standards are jurisdiction- and product-category-specific. Verify current editions and applicability in the relevant official standards catalog before using a standard as a compliance or test-plan basis.

## FURNIX rule model and validation workflow

### Takeaway
Keep FURNIX projects router/CNC-agnostic by storing **abstract, sourced machining operations** in part-local coordinates. Gate manufacturing outputs on explicit rule and target-machine validation; an absent or approximate operation must remain visibly unresolved, not be interpreted as “no drilling needed” or as a production-ready result.

### Cited findings
- FURNIX’s existing project notes already distinguish its user component catalog from a verified technical library: newly entered components do not currently create holes, and the CSV component list is not a drilling map. [FURNIX README](file:///C:/Users/%D0%90%D0%BD%D0%B4%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B9/Desktop/%D0%A1%D0%90%D0%99%D0%A2%20%D0%A0%D0%B0%D0%B9%20%D0%B8%20%D0%90%D0%B4/%D0%90%D1%80%D1%85%D0%B8%D0%B2/furnix/README.md)
- FURNIX also describes its current DXF as a documentation export that does not contain drilling and is not a CNC-ready file. [FURNIX README](file:///C:/Users/%D0%90%D0%BD%D0%B4%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B9/Desktop/%D0%A1%D0%90%D0%99%D0%A2%20%D0%A0%D0%B0%D0%B9%20%D0%B8%20%D0%90%D0%B4/%D0%90%D1%80%D1%85%D0%B8%D0%B2/furnix/README.md); [FURNIX roadmap](file:///C:/Users/%D0%90%D0%BD%D0%B4%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B9/Desktop/%D0%A1%D0%90%D0%99%D0%A2%20%D0%A0%D0%B0%D0%B9%20%D0%B8%20%D0%90%D0%B4/%D0%A0%D0%9E%D0%90%D0%94%D0%9C%D0%90%D0%9F.md)
- Manufacturer sources distinguish drawings, fitting data, drilling positions, CAM macros, and machine-oriented outputs; standards sources distinguish product-data exchange from NC program formatting. These distinctions support keeping FURNIX’s semantic model separate from export/postprocessor formats. [Blum FAQ](https://www.blum.com/eu/en/services/faq/); [Blum CAD/CAM Data Service](https://www.blum.com/eu/en/services/industrial-production/cad-cam-dataservice/); [ISO 10303-21](https://www.iso.org/standard/63141.html); [ISO 6983-1](https://www.iso.org/standard/34608.html)

### Inferences
**Recommended rule model**
- Represent each operation explicitly: stable operation ID; target part; operation kind (for example bore, through-bore, counterbore, slot, or pocket); part-local face; coordinate frame and origin; X/Y location; axis/direction; diameter/width; depth or through-condition; and any required fitting/tool parameters.
- Represent placement as a rule or parametric anchor (such as a specified edge, face, datum, or fitting reference), rather than only a fixed world-space point. Record handedness/mirroring and the transform from assembly coordinates to part-face coordinates.
- Attach provenance to every imported rule: manufacturer, product/SKU and variant, source file or URL, revision/date, locale, import method, and any FURNIX rule-set version. Preserve the original source payload where licensing permits.
- Keep a clear status per part and per operation: **unresolved**, **schematic**, **supplier-rule sourced**, **geometrically checked**, and **target-machine validated**. Do not let a successful import or DXF export promote a record to a stronger status.
- Treat FURNIX JSON as the project’s semantic source of truth. Offer CSV with a versioned, documented schema for tabular exchange; DXF/SVG/PDF for drawings and human-readable maps; STEP for supported geometry exchange; and WOP/CNC output only through a separately validated target-specific processor.
- Show a warning whenever holes or machining data are unknown. “No operations recorded” must not mean “confirmed no operations required.”
- Do not silently assume a generic 32-mm shelf-hole pattern, edge distance, drilling depth, or fitting geometry. Such rules must come from the selected supplier data, an explicitly selected documented rule set, or a clearly labeled user entry.

**Recommended validation workflow**
1. **Check source and scope:** confirm product/article number, variant, applicable mounting situation, source revision, and licensing/usage permissions.
2. **Validate imported data:** require known units, operation type, face, coordinate origin/axes, and depth semantics. Reject or flag incomplete mappings rather than guessing.
3. **Resolve geometry:** map the fitting’s local references to the actual panel; check mirror/handedness, panel thickness, edge clearance, duplicate holes, operation bounds, and collisions with other parts or fittings. Supplier-specific limits must come from supplier rules, not generic estimates.
4. **Compare against reference cases:** maintain versioned “golden” examples from authoritative vendor drawings/files. Test parsing, parameter changes, transforms, and exports against those expected operations.
5. **Validate manufacturing separately:** only for a named machine/controller and setup, use its approved postprocessor and tool/workholding configuration; simulate or backplot, then perform a controlled test on a sample part and inspect it before release.
6. **Version and identify outputs:** include project/rule revision, fitting source/revision, units, coordinate conventions, postprocessor and target machine (if applicable), validation state, and approval date/operator on any production package.
7. **Use standards for their actual scope:** ISO 10303-21 and ISO 6983-1 inform exchange/program-format layers; they do not certify FURNIX’s drilling calculations. Select applicable furniture performance and machine-safety standards separately, using the official standards catalog for the market and product category.

### Gaps
- The specific rule fields and geometric checks above are a recommended FURNIX design, not requirements prescribed by Blum, Hettich, iFurn, or the cited ISO standards.
- A production-ready claim requires direct testing with the specific supplier data, CAD/CAM receiver, postprocessor, machine/controller, and tooling. None of the reviewed public sources establishes that FURNIX currently passes that validation.
